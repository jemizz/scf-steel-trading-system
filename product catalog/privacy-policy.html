Privacy Policy

Effective Date: 24 September 2026  ·  Last Updated: 24 September 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how SCF Steel Trading (“we,” “us,” or “the Company”) collects, uses, stores, and protects personal information when you send an inquiry through our website or mobile Platform. Our public catalog is for viewing and inquiry only. We do not accept online orders or online payments, and we do not create a public customer account when you browse products.

This Policy is written to help you understand your rights under Philippine law, especially the Data Privacy Act of 2012. It is a notice for users of our inquiry form and public pages. It is not a substitute for formal legal advice.

1. Personal Data we collect

We collect personal data relating to you (“Personal Data”) only when you choose to communicate with us through the inquiry form or another official contact channel. Browsing the product catalog does not require an account.

Personal Data you provide in an inquiry

If you submit the Contact / inquiry form, we collect the information you type into that form:

  • Name: your full name, so we know who to address when we reply.
  • Contact number: a mobile or landline number so our staff can call or text you about stock, price, pick-up, or fabrication details.
  • Product or service of interest: the item or job you are asking about (for example, flat bar, roofing sheet, cutting and bending, or gutter works).
  • Inquiry message: the description of your request, measurements, quantity, timeline, or other details you decide to share.

What we do not collect through the public site

  • No online payment data. We do not collect credit card numbers, CVV codes, e-wallet PINs, OTP codes, or online banking credentials. The Platform does not accept online payments.
  • No public customer account. You do not create a login to browse products. We do not ask for a password, date of birth, or profile photo on the inquiry form.
  • No government IDs for browsing. We do not require a passport, national ID, or similar document just to send an inquiry.

If you later buy from us in the physical store, or we issue a quotation, sales invoice, or fabrication job ticket, additional business records may be created offline. Those records follow the same duty of care described in this Policy and in Philippine record-keeping laws.

2. How we use Personal Data

We use inquiry Personal Data only for the purpose you submitted it: so SCF Steel Trading can understand your request and respond. We do not sell inquiry data. We do not use it for third-party advertising.

  • To answer your inquiry: a staff member may call, text, email, or message you on Facebook about availability, specifications, pick-up, or a quotation.
  • To prepare a non-binding quotation: your name, number, and product details help us check stock or fabrication capacity before we reply.
  • To avoid duplicate or incomplete follow-ups: we may keep the inquiry long enough to finish the conversation you started.
  • To protect the business and other users: we may review a message if we suspect spam, fraud, harassment, or a security incident.
  • To comply with law: we may keep or disclose an inquiry if a court, the National Privacy Commission, or another lawful authority requires it.

Submitting an inquiry is not an online order and does not reserve stock. We use your data to talk to you, not to charge a card or open a public account.

3. Laws that protect you

Your Personal Data is protected by several Philippine laws and regulations. The most important ones for this inquiry form are explained below in plain language.

Republic Act No. 10173 — Data Privacy Act of 2012 (DPA)

This is the main privacy statute in the Philippines. It says personal information may be processed only if the processing is allowed by law, and only for a declared, specified, and legitimate purpose. The DPA requires transparency, legitimate purpose, and proportionality. It also gives you rights to be informed, to access, to object, to correct, to erase or block, to data portability, to damages, and to file a complaint. Unauthorized processing, negligent handling, concealment of a breach, and malicious disclosure can lead to fines and imprisonment.

Implementing Rules and Regulations of RA 10173 (NPC IRR)

The National Privacy Commission (NPC) issued the IRR that explains how the DPA works in practice. The IRR describes lawful criteria for processing, security measures (organizational, physical, and technical), breach notification, and how data subjects can exercise their rights. We follow the IRR when we decide what to collect on the inquiry form, how long to keep it, and how to answer a privacy request.

1987 Constitution, Article III, Section 3

The Bill of Rights protects the privacy of communication and correspondence. This constitutional guarantee is why messages you send to a business are not treated as public property. Any limitation on that privacy must rest on law and due process, not on convenience.

Civil Code of the Philippines (Articles 19, 26, and 32)

Article 19 requires every person to act with justice, give everyone their due, and observe honesty and good faith. Article 26 protects dignity, personality, and privacy from meddling or prying. Article 32 allows a civil action when privacy or other constitutional rights are violated. These rules sit beside the DPA and can support a claim for damages if personal data is abused.

Republic Act No. 8792 — Electronic Commerce Act of 2000

The E-Commerce Act recognizes electronic documents and electronic data messages, and it requires parties who handle electronic data to keep that information secure and reliable. Because your inquiry arrives through a website form or email, this law supports the duty to treat that message as a protected electronic record.

Republic Act No. 10175 — Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012

This law punishes illegal access, data interference, system interference, and misuse of devices. It does not replace the DPA. It adds criminal consequences if someone hacks the Platform or tampers with inquiry records.

NPC circulars on security and breach notification

The NPC issues circulars on security measures and on personal data breach management. If a breach involving your inquiry data creates a real risk of serious harm, the DPA and NPC rules require notice to the NPC and, in proper cases, to affected individuals. You may also complain directly to the NPC at privacy.gov.ph.

Together, these laws mean we should collect only what the inquiry needs, use it only to answer you, keep it secure, delete it when it is no longer needed, and respect the rights listed in Section 7.

4. Legal bases for processing

Under Section 12 of the Data Privacy Act and the NPC IRR, processing of personal information must rest on a lawful criterion. For the inquiry form we rely on the following:

  • Consent. You choose to type your name, number, and message and press send. That act is consent to be contacted about that specific inquiry. Consent should be freely given, specific, and informed. You may withdraw it by asking us to stop follow-up and to delete the inquiry, subject to any record we must keep by law.
  • Contract or pre-contract steps. If your message asks for a quotation or fabrication estimate, processing is also needed to take steps at your request before a possible sale. Sending an inquiry still does not bind you to buy.
  • Legitimate interests. We have a legitimate interest in answering customers, preventing spam, and keeping basic records of a conversation we already started. That interest is balanced against your rights. We do not treat “legitimate interest” as a license to market your number to other companies.
  • Legal obligation. If a lawful order or a tax or regulatory duty requires us to keep a record, we will keep only what that duty requires.

We do not process sensitive personal information (such as health data, government ID numbers, or religious affiliation) through the public inquiry form, and we ask that you do not include those details in your message unless they are truly necessary for the job you are requesting.

5. Disclosure of Personal Data

We disclose inquiry Personal Data only when it is necessary to answer you or when the law requires it.

  • Authorized staff of SCF Steel Trading. The person who reads inquiries, checks stock, or prepares a quotation may see your name, number, and message.
  • Service providers who run the form. Hosting, email delivery, or similar vendors may process the message as our processors. They are allowed to handle it only to deliver the service, not to use it for their own marketing.
  • Government authorities. We may disclose data if required by a court order, a lawful NPC process, or another legal duty.
  • Professional advisers. An accountant or counsel bound by confidentiality may see a record if a dispute or compliance review needs it.

We do not sell, rent, or trade inquiry lists. We do not publish your number on the public catalog.

6. Retention

The Data Privacy Act requires that personal data be kept only as long as necessary for the declared purpose, or as required by law. For inquiries:

  • Open inquiries. We keep the record while we are still answering you.
  • Closed inquiries that did not become a sale. We generally delete or anonymize them within twelve (12) months after the last response, unless you ask us to delete sooner and no legal hold applies.
  • Inquiries that become a quotation, sale, or fabrication job. The needed details may move into a business record and then follow tax and accounting retention rules, which in the Philippines commonly last at least five (5) years, or longer if a dispute remains open.
  • Security logs. Technical logs that are not the inquiry itself are usually kept for a short period (typically up to ninety (90) days) unless an investigation needs them.

7. Your rights

As a data subject under RA 10173 and the NPC IRR, you have the following rights. Some rights have legal limits — for example, we cannot erase a record that tax law still requires us to keep.

  • Right to be informed. This Policy is the notice of what we collect and why.
  • Right to access. You may ask whether we hold an inquiry from you and request a copy of the Personal Data in it.
  • Right to object. You may tell us to stop using your number for follow-up on that inquiry.
  • Right to rectification. You may ask us to correct a wrong name, number, or other detail.
  • Right to erasure or blocking. You may ask us to delete or suspend the inquiry when it is no longer needed, when consent is withdrawn and no other lawful basis remains, or when the data was processed unlawfully.
  • Right to data portability. Where technically feasible, you may ask for the data you gave us in a commonly used electronic format.
  • Right to damages. The DPA and the Civil Code allow you to claim damages if your privacy rights are violated, subject to proof and legal process.
  • Right to file a complaint. You may complain to SCF Steel Trading first, and you may also complain to the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph).

To use these rights, send a request using the details in Section 13. Please use the subject line “Data Privacy Request,” and include your name, the number you used in the inquiry, and what you want us to do. We may need to verify that you are the same person who sent the inquiry before we release or delete data. That check protects you from someone else pretending to be you.

8. Data controls

You already control most of what we receive, because we only get what you type into the form.

  • Do not include extra sensitive details (IDs, card numbers, medical notes) in the message unless they are truly required for the job.
  • You may ask us to use email only, or phone only, after the first reply.
  • You may ask us to delete a closed inquiry that did not become a transaction.
  • You may withdraw consent to further contact at any time.

9. Security

The DPA requires personal information controllers to implement reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical, and technical security measures. For inquiry data we aim to:

  • Limit access to staff who need the message to reply.
  • Use HTTPS/TLS on the website where the hosting environment supports it, so the form is encrypted in transit.
  • Avoid storing payment credentials, because we do not take online payments.
  • Review and remove inquiry records when the retention period ends.

No electronic form is perfectly secure. If we become aware of a personal data breach that meets the NPC threshold for notification, we will follow the DPA and NPC breach rules.

10. Children

The inquiry form is meant for adult customers, contractors, and business buyers. We do not knowingly collect Personal Data from children under thirteen (13) through this form. If a parent or guardian believes a child sent us an inquiry, contact us and we will delete it.

11. Cookies and device data

The public pages may use essential cookies or similar storage so the site loads correctly. We do not use the inquiry form to run third-party advertising cookies. Your browser can block cookies; some pages may then work less smoothly. Technical data such as IP address or browser type may be recorded by the server for security and uptime. That technical data is not used to build a marketing profile of catalog visitors.

12. Changes to this Privacy Policy

We may update this Policy if the form, our vendors, or the law changes. The Effective Date and Last Updated date at the top will change when we do. The current version will be posted on this page. If a change is material, we will also note it in the website footer or in the reply we send to new inquiries.
